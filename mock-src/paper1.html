Question 1 — Normal distribution 5 marks
Working backwards from percentages to μ and σ

The daily wages of workers in a large factory are normally distributed. It is found that 10% of the workers earn less than ₹200 per day, while 20% of them earn more than ₹500 per day.

Find the mean and the standard deviation of the daily wage, and hence the percentage of workers earning between ₹300 and ₹400 per day.

Hint: each percentage gives you one z-value. Two z-values, two unknowns.

  1. Turn each percentage into a z. $P(X < 200) = 0.10$, so the area to the left is 0.10 and from the Z-table $z_1 = -1.28$. $P(X > 500) = 0.20$ means $P(X < 500) = 0.80$, so $z_2 = 0.84$.
  2. Write the two standardising equations. $$\frac{200-\mu}{\sigma} = -1.28 \qquad \frac{500-\mu}{\sigma} = 0.84$$
  3. Subtract to kill μ. $\dfrac{500-200}{\sigma} = 0.84-(-1.28) = 2.12$, so $\sigma = \dfrac{300}{2.12} = 141.5094$.
  4. Back-substitute for μ. $\mu = 200 + 1.28(141.5094) = 381.1321$.
  5. Now the band 300 to 400. $z_{300} = \dfrac{300-381.1321}{141.5094} = -0.57$ and $z_{400} = \dfrac{400-381.1321}{141.5094} = 0.13$.
  6. Read the table and subtract. $\Phi(0.13) = 0.5517$, $\Phi(-0.57) = 0.2843$, so $P = 0.5517 - 0.2843 = 0.2674$.
μ = ₹381.1321 · σ = ₹141.5094 · 26.74% of workers earn between ₹300 and ₹400
Question 2 — Chebyshev's inequality 5 marks
Bounding a probability with no distribution assumed

A fair spinner is marked 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and each number is equally likely. Let $X$ be the number obtained in one spin, with mean $\mu$.

Show that Chebyshev's inequality gives $P\{|X-\mu| \ge 2\} \le 0.5$, and compare this bound with the exact probability. Comment on what the comparison shows.

  1. Mean. $\mu = E(X) = \dfrac{1+2+3+4+5}{5} = 3$.
  2. Variance. $E(X^2) = \dfrac{1+4+9+16+25}{5} = \dfrac{55}{5} = 11$, so $\sigma^2 = E(X^2) - \mu^2 = 11 - 9 = 2$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{2} = 1.4142$.
  3. Apply Chebyshev. In the form $P\{|X-\mu| \ge c\} \le \dfrac{\sigma^2}{c^2}$ with $c = 2$: $$P\{|X-3| \ge 2\} \le \frac{2}{2^2} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5000$$
  4. Exact probability. $|X-3| \ge 2$ happens only when $X = 1$ or $X = 5$, so the exact probability is $\dfrac{2}{5} = 0.4000$.
  5. Comment. $0.4000 \le 0.5000$, so the inequality holds. The bound is correct but loose — that is the price of using only μ and σ and assuming nothing about the shape of the distribution. Chebyshev never promises a tight answer, only a guaranteed ceiling.
Chebyshev bound = 0.5000 · exact = 0.4000 · the bound holds and is loose
Question 3 — Sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$ 2+2+2 marks
Central Limit Theorem applied to a sample mean

A café serves on average 240 customers per day. Assume the population standard deviation is 45 customers. A random sample of 36 days is taken.

a. What is the probability that the sample mean is more than 252 customers?

b. What is the probability that the sample mean is less than 231 customers?

c. What is the probability that the sample mean is between 234 and 249 customers?

  1. Standard error first — it is used in all three parts. $n = 36 \ge 30$, so by the Central Limit Theorem $\bar{X}$ is approximately normal with $$\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{45}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{45}{6} = 7.5$$
  2. (a) $z = \dfrac{252-240}{7.5} = 1.60$. From the table $\Phi(1.60) = 0.9452$, so $P(\bar{X} > 252) = 1 - 0.9452 = 0.0548$.
  3. (b) $z = \dfrac{231-240}{7.5} = -1.20$, giving $P(\bar{X} < 231) = \Phi(-1.20) = 0.1151$.
  4. (c) $z_1 = \dfrac{234-240}{7.5} = -0.80$ and $z_2 = \dfrac{249-240}{7.5} = 1.20$. Then $P = \Phi(1.20) - \Phi(-0.80) = 0.8849 - 0.2119 = 0.6730$.
a. 0.0548 · b. 0.1151 · c. 0.6730
Question 4 — Confidence interval for μ 6 marks
σ unknown, small sample — the t-distribution

Let $\{12,\ 15,\ 11,\ 18,\ 14,\ 16,\ 13,\ 17\}$ be a random sample drawn from a $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ distribution, where both $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are unknown.

Find the 95% confidence interval for $\mu$.

  1. Sample mean. $\sum x = 116$, $n = 8$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{116}{8} = 14.5$.
  2. Sample variance. The deviations are $-2.5, 0.5, -3.5, 3.5, -0.5, 1.5, -1.5, 2.5$ and their squares sum to $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 42$. Therefore $$s^2 = \frac{42}{8-1} = 6 \qquad s = \sqrt{6} = 2.4495$$
  3. Choose the tool. σ is unknown and $n = 8$ is small, so use $t$ with $n-1 = 7$ degrees of freedom, not $z$.
  4. Critical value. For 95% two-sided, $t_{0.025,\,7} = 2.365$.
  5. Standard error and margin. $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{2.4495}{\sqrt{8}} = 0.8660$, so the margin of error is $2.365 \times 0.8660 = 2.0482$.
  6. Assemble. $14.5 \pm 2.0482$.
95% CI for μ = (12.4518, 16.5482)
Question 5 — Test for a proportion 7 marks
p-value method, one-tailed

An online pharmacy claimed that at least 85% of its orders are delivered within 24 hours. However, a random sample of 250 orders showed that only 198 were delivered within 24 hours.

Test the claim of the pharmacy at 5% level of significance by using the p-value method.

  1. Hypotheses. The claim is the status quo, so it goes in $H_0$. The sample is below the claim, so the alternative points left: $$H_0: p \ge 0.85 \qquad H_1: p < 0.85$$
  2. Sample proportion. $\hat{p} = \dfrac{198}{250} = 0.7920$.
  3. Standard error under $H_0$. Always use the hypothesised $p_0$, never $\hat{p}$: $$\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.85 \times 0.15}{250}} = \sqrt{0.00051} = 0.0226$$
  4. Test statistic. $z = \dfrac{0.7920 - 0.85}{0.022583} = \dfrac{-0.0580}{0.022583} = -2.5683$, which you look up as $-2.57$.
  5. p-value. Left-tailed, so the p-value is the whole left tail: $P(Z < -2.57) = 0.0051$.
  6. Decide. $0.0051 < 0.05$, so reject $H_0$ at the 5% level.
  7. Conclude in words. There is sufficient evidence at the 5% level that fewer than 85% of orders arrive within 24 hours. The pharmacy's claim is not supported.
z = −2.5683 · p-value = 0.0051 < 0.05 · reject H₀, the claim is not supported
Question 6 — Test for a population variance 6 marks
Chi-square test, two-tailed

The marks obtained by 10 students in a class test are given below.

12151714 16131815 1416

Can we say that the population variance of the distribution of marks, from which the above sample of 10 students was drawn, is equal to 2? Test an appropriate hypothesis at 5% level.

  1. Hypotheses. "Is it equal to 2" is a two-tailed question: $$H_0: \sigma^2 = 2 \qquad H_1: \sigma^2 \ne 2$$
  2. Sample mean. $\sum x = 150$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{150}{10} = 15$.
  3. Sum of squared deviations. The deviations are $-3, 0, 2, -1, 1, -2, 3, 0, -1, 1$ and their squares are $9, 0, 4, 1, 1, 4, 9, 0, 1, 1$, so $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 30$ and $s^2 = \dfrac{30}{9} = 3.3333$.
  4. Test statistic. $$\chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} = \frac{30}{2} = 15.0000 \qquad \text{with } df = 9$$
  5. Critical values. Two-tailed at 5% splits α into 0.025 each side: $\chi^2_{0.975,\,9} = 2.700$ and $\chi^2_{0.025,\,9} = 19.023$.
  6. Decide. $2.700 < 15.0000 < 19.023$, so the statistic falls in the acceptance region. Do not reject $H_0$.
  7. Conclude. At the 5% level there is no evidence against the claim that the population variance of marks equals 2.
χ² = 15.0000, df = 9 · acceptance region (2.700, 19.023) · do not reject H₀

Question 8 — Regression and correlation 2+2+2+2 marks
Recovering everything from two regression lines

In a partially destroyed laboratory record of an analysis of bivariate data, the following results only are legible.

Legible $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 25$, and the two regression lines are $$24X - 25Y + 260 = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 8X - 3Y = 20$$

a. The mean values of $X$ and $Y$.

b. Which line is which, with justification.

c. The coefficient of correlation between $X$ and $Y$.

d. The standard deviation of $Y$.

  1. (a) Both regression lines pass through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. So solve them simultaneously. From the second, $8\bar{x} = 3\bar{y} + 20$, i.e. $\bar{x} = \dfrac{3\bar{y}+20}{8}$. Substituting into the first: $$24\left(\frac{3\bar{y}+20}{8}\right) - 25\bar{y} + 260 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 3(3\bar{y}+20) - 25\bar{y} + 260 = 0$$ $$9\bar{y} + 60 - 25\bar{y} + 260 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; -16\bar{y} = -320 \;\Rightarrow\; \bar{y} = 20$$ and then $\bar{x} = \dfrac{3(20)+20}{8} = \dfrac{80}{8} = 10$.
  2. (b) Test the assignment with $r^2 = b_{yx}\,b_{xy} \le 1$. Take the first line as $Y$ on $X$: $25Y = 24X + 260$ gives $b_{yx} = \dfrac{24}{25} = 0.96$. Take the second as $X$ on $Y$: $8X = 3Y + 20$ gives $b_{xy} = \dfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$. Then $r^2 = 0.96 \times 0.375 = 0.36 \le 1$ — valid.
  3. Check the other way round fails. If the first were $X$ on $Y$ then $b_{xy} = \dfrac{25}{24}$, and the second as $Y$ on $X$ gives $b_{yx} = \dfrac{8}{3}$, so $r^2 = \dfrac{25}{24} \times \dfrac{8}{3} = 2.7778 > 1$, which is impossible. The first assignment is therefore the correct one.
  4. (c) Correlation. $r = \pm\sqrt{0.36} = \pm 0.6$. Both slopes are positive, so $r$ takes the positive sign: $r = 0.6000$.
  5. (d) Standard deviation of Y. $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 25$ gives $\sigma_x = 5$. From $b_{yx} = r\dfrac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$: $$0.96 = 0.6 \times \frac{\sigma_y}{5} \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma_y = \frac{0.96 \times 5}{0.6} = 8.0000$$
a. x̄ = 10, ȳ = 20 · b. 24X−25Y+260=0 is Y on X · c. r = 0.6000 · d. σ_y = 8.0000
