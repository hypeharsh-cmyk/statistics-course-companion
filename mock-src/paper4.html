Question 1 — Normal distribution 5 marks
Moderate Classification into divisions — his exact Q1 pattern

In an examination it is laid down that a student passes if he secures 40 percent or more marks. He is placed in the first, second or third division according as he secures 60% or more marks, between 45% and 60% marks, and marks between 40% and 45% respectively. He gets distinction in case he secures 75% or more marks. It is noticed from the result that 10% of the students failed in the examination, whereas 5% of them obtained distinction.

Calculate the percentage of students placed in the second division. (Assume the normal distribution of marks.)

Hint: the failure rate and the distinction rate are your two equations. Nothing can be computed until μ and σ are out.

  1. Turn the two given percentages into z-values. Failing means scoring under 40, so $P(X < 40) = 0.10$ and $z_1 = -1.28$. Distinction means 75 or more, so $P(X > 75) = 0.05$, i.e. $P(X < 75) = 0.95$ and $z_2 = 1.645$.
  2. Write both standardising equations. $$\frac{40-\mu}{\sigma} = -1.28 \qquad \frac{75-\mu}{\sigma} = 1.645$$
  3. Subtract to eliminate μ. $$\frac{75-40}{\sigma} = 1.645 - (-1.28) = 2.925 \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma = \frac{35}{2.925} = 11.9658$$
  4. Back-substitute for μ. $\mu = 40 + 1.28(11.9658) = 40 + 15.3162 = 55.3162$.
  5. Second division is the band $45 \le X < 60$. $$z_{45} = \frac{45-55.3162}{11.9658} = -0.86 \qquad z_{60} = \frac{60-55.3162}{11.9658} = 0.39$$
  6. Read the table and subtract. $\Phi(0.39) = 0.6517$ and $\Phi(-0.86) = 0.1949$, so $$P(45 \le X < 60) = 0.6517 - 0.1949 = 0.4568$$
  7. Sense-check with the other divisions. First division $= 1 - 0.6517 = 0.3483$, third $= 0.1949 - 0.1000 = 0.0949$, failed $= 0.1000$. Adding: $0.3483 + 0.4568 + 0.0949 + 0.1000 = 1.0000$ ✓
μ = 55.3162, σ = 11.9658 · 45.68% of students are placed in the second division
Question 2 — Chebyshev's inequality 5 marks
Moderate A "show that" proof, exactly as he set it

If $X$ is the random variable denoting the number scored in a throw of a fair die, show that Chebyshev's inequality gives

$$P\{|X-\mu| \ge 2\} \le 0.7292,$$

where $\mu$ is the mean of $X$. Compare the bound with the exact probability and comment.

  1. Mean of a fair die. $\mu = E(X) = \dfrac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = \dfrac{21}{6} = 3.5$.
  2. Second moment. $E(X^2) = \dfrac{1+4+9+16+25+36}{6} = \dfrac{91}{6} = 15.1667$.
  3. Variance. $\sigma^2 = E(X^2) - \mu^2 = 15.1667 - (3.5)^2 = 15.1667 - 12.25 = 2.9167$.
  4. Apply Chebyshev in the form $P\{|X-\mu| \ge c\} \le \dfrac{\sigma^2}{c^2}$ with $c = 2$: $$P\{|X-3.5| \ge 2\} \le \frac{2.9167}{4} = 0.7292 \qquad \blacksquare$$
  5. Exact probability. $|X-3.5| \ge 2$ means $X \le 1.5$ or $X \ge 5.5$, and since $X$ takes only whole values that is $X \in \{1, 6\}$: $$P = \frac{2}{6} = 0.3333$$
  6. Comment. $0.3333 \le 0.7292$, so the inequality holds — and it is loose by more than a factor of two. That is expected: Chebyshev is built to hold for every distribution with this mean and variance, so it must leave room for far nastier ones than a die. It guarantees a ceiling, never an estimate.
σ² = 2.9167 · Chebyshev bound = 0.7292 · exact = 0.3333 · bound holds, and is loose
Question 3 — Sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$ 2+2+2 marks
Easy Straight CLT — the guaranteed marks on this paper

According to a telecom operator, customer-service calls last on average 4.5 minutes. Assume the population standard deviation is 1.2 minutes. Suppose a random sample of 36 calls is taken.

a. What is the probability that the sample mean is more than 4.8 minutes?

b. What is the probability that the sample mean is less than 4.2 minutes?

c. What is the probability that the sample mean is between 4.3 and 4.9 minutes?

  1. Standard error once, used three times. $n = 36 \ge 30$, so by the Central Limit Theorem $\bar{X}$ is approximately normal with $$\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1.2}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{1.2}{6} = 0.2$$
  2. (a) $z = \dfrac{4.8-4.5}{0.2} = 1.50$, so $P(\bar{X} > 4.8) = 1 - \Phi(1.50) = 1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668$.
  3. (b) $z = \dfrac{4.2-4.5}{0.2} = -1.50$, so $P(\bar{X} < 4.2) = \Phi(-1.50) = 0.0668$. (Same answer as (a), by symmetry — 4.2 and 4.8 are equidistant from 4.5.)
  4. (c) $z_1 = \dfrac{4.3-4.5}{0.2} = -1.00$ and $z_2 = \dfrac{4.9-4.5}{0.2} = 2.00$, so $$P = \Phi(2.00) - \Phi(-1.00) = 0.9772 - 0.1587 = 0.8185$$
a. 0.0668 · b. 0.0668 · c. 0.8185
Question 4 — Confidence interval for μ 6 marks
Easy–Moderate σ unknown, small sample — the t-interval

Let $\{3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9,\ 11,\ 13,\ 15,\ 17\}$ be the set of collected random sample values from a $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ distribution, where both $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are unknown.

Find the 95% confidence interval for $\mu$.

Hint: σ is unknown, so it is t, not z — and the degrees of freedom are $n-1$.

  1. Sample mean. $\sum x = 80$ and $n = 8$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{80}{8} = 10$.
  2. Sample variance. The deviations are $-7,-5,-3,-1,1,3,5,7$, whose squares are $49,25,9,1,1,9,25,49$ and sum to $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 168$. Therefore $$s^2 = \frac{168}{8-1} = 24 \qquad s = \sqrt{24} = 4.8990$$
  3. Choose the tool. $\sigma$ is unknown and $n = 8$ is small, so use $t$ with $df = n-1 = 7$.
  4. Critical value. For a 95% two-sided interval, $t_{0.025,\,7} = 2.365$.
  5. Standard error and margin of error. $$\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{4.8990}{\sqrt{8}} = 1.7321 \qquad ME = 2.365 \times 1.7321 = 4.0963$$
  6. Assemble. $10 \pm 4.0963$.
95% CI for μ = (5.9037, 14.0963)
Question 5 — Proportion test, then its Type II error 4+3 marks
(a) Moderate (b) Hard p-value method, then the probability of missing a real shortfall

A supplier claimed that at least 92% of the components which he supplied are conforming to the specifications. However, a random sample of 250 components showed only 220 were conforming to the specifications.

a. Test the claim of the supplier at 5% level of significance by using the p-value method. (4)

b. Using the same test, find the probability of a Type II error if the true conforming rate is in fact 88%, and state the power of the test. (3)

  1. (a) Hypotheses. "At least 92%" carries the equality, so it is the null. The sample falls short of the claim, so the alternative points left: $$H_0: p \ge 0.92 \qquad H_1: p < 0.92$$
  2. Sample proportion. $\hat{p} = \dfrac{220}{250} = 0.8800$.
  3. Standard error under $H_0$ — use $p_0$, never $\hat{p}$. $$\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.92 \times 0.08}{250}} = \sqrt{0.0002944} = 0.017158$$
  4. Test statistic. $z = \dfrac{0.8800-0.9200}{0.017158} = \dfrac{-0.0400}{0.017158} = -2.3313$, read as $-2.33$.
  5. p-value. The test is left-tailed, so the p-value is the whole left tail: $P(Z < -2.33) = 0.0099$.
  6. Decide and conclude. $0.0099 < 0.05$, so reject $H_0$ at the 5% level. There is sufficient evidence that fewer than 92% of components conform; the supplier's claim is not supported.
  7. (b) Find the decision boundary in $\hat{p}$ units. Left-tailed at $\alpha = 0.05$ uses $z = -1.645$, so we reject only when $$\hat{p} < 0.92 - 1.645(0.017158) = 0.92 - 0.028225 = 0.8918$$
  8. Re-centre on the true value $p = 0.88$. The standard error changes with $p$: $$\sigma_{\hat{p}}' = \sqrt{\frac{0.88 \times 0.12}{250}} = \sqrt{0.0004224} = 0.020552$$
  9. β is the chance of landing in the non-rejection region. $$\beta = P(\hat{p} > 0.8918 \mid p = 0.88) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\frac{0.8918-0.88}{0.020552}\right) = 1 - \Phi(0.57) = 1 - 0.7157 = 0.2843$$
  10. Power. $1 - \beta = 0.7157$. So even though the true rate really is 88%, this test would fail to detect it about 28% of the time.
a. z = −2.3313, p-value = 0.0099 < 0.05, reject H₀ · b. β = 0.2843, power = 0.7157
Question 6 — Variance test, cross-checked by an interval 4+2 marks
Hard A χ² test, then the CI that must agree with it

The lifetimes (in hours) of 9 randomly chosen bulbs from a large consignment are given below.

10109951005 10009901015 9851008992

a. Can we say that the population variance of the distribution of lifetimes, from which the above sample of 9 bulbs was drawn, is equal to 100 square hours? Test an appropriate hypothesis at 5% level. (4)

b. Construct the 95% confidence interval for $\sigma^2$ and show that it agrees with your decision in part (a). (2)

  1. (a) Hypotheses. "Is it equal to 100" is non-directional, so the test is two-tailed: $$H_0: \sigma^2 = 100 \qquad H_1: \sigma^2 \ne 100$$
  2. Sample mean. $\sum x = 9000$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{9000}{9} = 1000$.
  3. Sum of squared deviations. The deviations from 1000 are $10, -5, 5, 0, -10, 15, -15, 8, -8$, whose squares are $100, 25, 25, 0, 100, 225, 225, 64, 64$, giving $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 828$ and $$s^2 = \frac{828}{8} = 103.5$$
  4. Test statistic. $$\chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} = \frac{828}{100} = 8.2800 \qquad df = 8$$
  5. Critical values. Two-tailed at 5% puts 0.025 in each tail, so with $df = 8$: $\chi^2_{0.975,\,8} = 2.180$ and $\chi^2_{0.025,\,8} = 17.535$.
  6. Decide. $2.180 < 8.2800 < 17.535$, so the statistic lies in the non-rejection region. Do not reject $H_0$. At the 5% level there is no evidence against the claim that the population variance equals 100 square hours.
  7. (b) Build the interval from the same two critical values. $$\left(\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{0.025,\,8}},\ \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{0.975,\,8}}\right) = \left(\frac{828}{17.535},\ \frac{828}{2.180}\right) = (47.2199,\ 379.8165)$$
  8. Show the agreement. The hypothesised value 100 lies inside $(47.2199, 379.8165)$, and a two-tailed test at 5% rejects exactly when the hypothesised value falls outside the 95% interval. Both routes therefore give the same verdict: do not reject. Note also how wide the interval is — nine observations say very little about a variance.
a. χ² = 8.2800, df = 8, non-rejection region (2.180, 17.535) → do not reject H₀ · b. 95% CI for σ² = (47.2199, 379.8165), which contains 100 ✓

Find the rank correlation coefficient between the Advertisement Cost and Sales from the data given below.

Question 8 — Regression and correlation 2+2+2+2 marks
Hard Two lines in, everything out — then a prediction

In a partially destroyed laboratory record of an analysis of bivariate data the following results only are legible.

Legible $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 9$, and the two regression lines are $$2X - 3Y + 12 = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 4X - 5Y + 6 = 0$$

a. The mean values of $X$ and $Y$.

b. Which line is the regression of $Y$ on $X$, with justification, and the coefficient of correlation between $X$ and $Y$.

c. The standard deviation of $Y$.

d. Estimate $Y$ when $X = 24$, stating which line you must use and why.

  1. (a) Both regression lines pass through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$, so the means are the simultaneous solution. From the first, $2\bar{x} = 3\bar{y} - 12$. Substituting into the second: $$2(3\bar{y}-12) - 5\bar{y} + 6 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 6\bar{y} - 24 - 5\bar{y} + 6 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \bar{y} = 18$$ and then $\bar{x} = \dfrac{3(18)-12}{2} = \dfrac{42}{2} = 21$. Check in the second line: $4(21) - 5(18) + 6 = 84 - 90 + 6 = 0$ ✓
  2. (b) Decide the assignment using $r^2 = b_{yx}\,b_{xy} \le 1$. Try the first line as $Y$ on $X$: $3Y = 2X + 12$ gives $b_{yx} = \dfrac{2}{3} = 0.6667$. Then the second as $X$ on $Y$: $4X = 5Y - 6$ gives $b_{xy} = \dfrac{5}{4} = 1.25$. $$r^2 = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{5}{6} = 0.8333 \le 1 \quad\text{— valid}$$
  3. Confirm the other assignment is impossible. If the first were $X$ on $Y$ then $b_{xy} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, and the second as $Y$ on $X$ gives $b_{yx} = \dfrac{4}{5}$, so $r^2 = 1.5 \times 0.8 = 1.2 > 1$ — impossible. So $2X - 3Y + 12 = 0$ is the regression of $Y$ on $X$.
  4. Correlation. $r = \pm\sqrt{0.8333} = \pm 0.9129$, and both regression coefficients are positive, so $r$ takes the positive sign: $r = 0.9129$.
  5. (c) Standard deviation of Y. $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 9$ gives $\sigma_x = 3$. From $b_{yx} = r\dfrac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$: $$\frac{2}{3} = 0.9129 \times \frac{\sigma_y}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma_y = \frac{2}{0.9129} = 2.1909$$ Check with the other coefficient: $b_{xy} = r\dfrac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = 0.9129 \times \dfrac{3}{2.1909} = 1.25$ ✓
  6. (d) Choose the line by what is being predicted. We are given $X$ and want $Y$, so we must use the regression of $Y$ on $X$ — the line that minimises vertical deviations. That is $2X - 3Y + 12 = 0$. Using the other line here would be a straightforward error.
  7. Substitute. $$Y = \frac{2X+12}{3} = \frac{2(24)+12}{3} = \frac{60}{3} = 20.0000$$
a. x̄ = 21, ȳ = 18 · b. 2X−3Y+12=0 is Y on X, r = 0.9129 · c. σ_y = 2.1909 · d. Ŷ = 20.0000
