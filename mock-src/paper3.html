Question 1 — Normal distribution 2+1+2 marks
μ and σ recovered, then a guarantee time and a conditional probability

The lifetime of an electronic component is normally distributed. It is found that 10% of the components last less than 1200 hours, while 5% of them last more than 1800 hours.

a. Find the mean and the standard deviation of the lifetime.

b. The manufacturer replaces free of charge any component that fails before a stated guarantee time. If at most 3% of components are to be replaced, what guarantee time should be stated?

c. Among the components that are still working at 1500 hours, what fraction will survive beyond 1800 hours?

Hint for (c): "among those that survived 1500" is the language of conditional probability.

  1. (a) Turn each percentage into a z. $P(X < 1200) = 0.10$ gives $z_1 = -1.28$, and $P(X > 1800) = 0.05$ gives $z_2 = 1.645$.
  2. Two equations, two unknowns. $$\frac{1200-\mu}{\sigma} = -1.28 \qquad \frac{1800-\mu}{\sigma} = 1.645$$ Subtracting, $\dfrac{600}{\sigma} = 2.925$, so $\sigma = 205.1282$ hours, and $\mu = 1200 + 1.28(205.1282) = 1462.5641$ hours.
  3. (b) Guarantee time. At most 3% replaced means the guarantee time $t$ has 3% of the area to its left, so $z = -1.88$: $$t = \mu + z\sigma = 1462.5641 - 1.88(205.1282) = 1462.5641 - 385.6410 = 1076.9231$$ Rounding down to be safe, state a guarantee of about 1076 hours.
  4. (c) Set up the conditional probability. $$P(X > 1800 \mid X > 1500) = \frac{P(X > 1800 \ \cap\ X > 1500)}{P(X > 1500)} = \frac{P(X > 1800)}{P(X > 1500)}$$ because surviving past 1800 already implies surviving past 1500.
  5. Numerator is given. $P(X > 1800) = 0.05$.
  6. Denominator. $z = \dfrac{1500-1462.5641}{205.1282} = 0.18$, so $\Phi(0.18) = 0.5714$ and $P(X > 1500) = 1 - 0.5714 = 0.4286$.
  7. Divide. $\dfrac{0.05}{0.4286} = 0.1167$.
a. μ = 1462.5641 h, σ = 205.1282 h · b. guarantee ≈ 1076.9231 h · c. 0.1167
Question 2 — Markov and Chebyshev compared 1+1+1+2 marks
Two bounds on the same event, and inverting one of them

The daily demand $X$ at a retail counter is a non-negative random variable with mean 60 units and variance 25 units². Nothing else is known about its distribution.

a. Use Markov's inequality to find an upper bound for $P(X \ge 80)$.

b. Use Chebyshev's inequality to find an upper bound for $P(X \ge 80)$.

c. Which bound is sharper, and why?

d. Find the smallest $c$ for which Chebyshev's inequality guarantees $P\{|X-\mu| < c\} \ge 0.96$.

  1. (a) Markov. For a non-negative $X$, $P(X \ge a) \le \dfrac{E(X)}{a}$: $$P(X \ge 80) \le \frac{60}{80} = 0.7500$$
  2. (b) Chebyshev. The event $X \ge 80$ sits inside the two-sided event $|X-60| \ge 20$, so $$P(X \ge 80) \le P\{|X-60| \ge 20\} \le \frac{\sigma^2}{20^2} = \frac{25}{400} = 0.0625$$
  3. (c) Which is sharper. Chebyshev, by a factor of twelve. Markov uses only the mean and so must allow for distributions that put a lot of probability far above 60. Chebyshev also knows the variance is only 25, i.e. $\sigma = 5$, and 80 is four standard deviations above the mean — information Markov simply cannot see. More information, tighter bound.
  4. (d) Invert the inequality. Chebyshev in complement form says $$P\{|X-\mu| < c\} \ge 1 - \frac{\sigma^2}{c^2}$$ so we need $1 - \dfrac{25}{c^2} \ge 0.96$.
  5. Solve. $\dfrac{25}{c^2} \le 0.04 \Rightarrow c^2 \ge \dfrac{25}{0.04} = 625 \Rightarrow c \ge 25$. The smallest such $c$ is 25 units.
  6. Sense-check. $c = 25$ is $k = 5$ standard deviations, and $1 - 1/k^2 = 1 - 1/25 = 0.96$ exactly, as required.
a. 0.7500 · b. 0.0625 · c. Chebyshev, it uses the variance · d. c = 25.0000
Question 3 — Sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$ 2+2+2 marks
A probability, a required sample size, and a finite population

A population has mean 75 and standard deviation 12.

a. For a random sample of 36, find the probability that the sample mean lies within 3 units of the population mean.

b. How large must the sample be so that the sample mean lies within 2 units of the population mean with probability 0.95?

c. Suppose instead the population contains only $N = 200$ members and a sample of $n = 50$ is drawn without replacement. Using the finite population correction $\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\dfrac{N-n}{N-1}}$, find $P(\bar{X} > 78)$.

  1. (a) Standard error. $\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{36}} = 2$. "Within 3 units" means $|\bar{X}-\mu| < 3$, i.e. $|z| < \dfrac{3}{2} = 1.50$.
  2. Two-sided area. $$P(|Z| < 1.50) = 2\Phi(1.50) - 1 = 2(0.9332) - 1 = 0.8664$$
  3. (b) Set the margin and solve for n. For probability 0.95 the z-value is 1.96, and the half-width must equal 2: $$1.96 \times \frac{12}{\sqrt{n}} = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; \sqrt{n} = \frac{1.96 \times 12}{2} = 11.76$$
  4. Square and round up. $n = (11.76)^2 = 138.2976$. Sample size must be a whole number and must not fall short, so round up: $n = 139$.
  5. (c) Apply the correction factor. $$\sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}} = \sqrt{\frac{200-50}{199}} = \sqrt{0.7538} = 0.8682$$ $$\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{50}} \times 0.8682 = 1.6971 \times 0.8682 = 1.4734$$
  6. Standardise and read. $z = \dfrac{78-75}{1.4734} = 2.04$, so $P(\bar{X} > 78) = 1 - \Phi(2.04) = 1 - 0.9793 = 0.0207$.
  7. Note why the correction matters. Without it the standard error would be 1.6971 and $z = 1.77$, giving 0.0384 — nearly double. Sampling a quarter of a small population really does make $\bar{X}$ less variable.
a. 0.8664 · b. n = 139 · c. 0.0207
Question 4 — Confidence intervals 2+2+2 marks
An interval for μ, an interval for σ², and a sample-size consequence

A random sample of 12 observations drawn from a normal population gives $\sum x = 300$ and $\sum x^2 = 7600$.

a. Construct a 99% confidence interval for $\mu$.

b. Construct a 95% confidence interval for $\sigma^2$.

c. Roughly what sample size would be needed to halve the width of the interval in part (a)?

  1. Mean and corrected sum of squares. $\bar{x} = \dfrac{300}{12} = 25$, and using the shortcut $$\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = \sum x^2 - n\bar{x}^2 = 7600 - 12(25)^2 = 7600 - 7500 = 100$$ so $s^2 = \dfrac{100}{11} = 9.0909$ and $s = 3.0151$.
  2. (a) σ is unknown, so use t with $df = 11$. For 99% two-sided, $t_{0.005,\,11} = 3.106$.
  3. Margin of error. $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{3.0151}{\sqrt{12}} = 0.8704$, so the margin is $3.106 \times 0.8704 = 2.7034$, giving $25 \pm 2.7034$.
  4. (b) Interval for a variance uses chi-square, and the tails swap sides. $$\left(\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{0.025,\,11}},\ \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{0.975,\,11}}\right)$$ with $\chi^2_{0.025,\,11} = 21.920$ and $\chi^2_{0.975,\,11} = 3.816$.
  5. Substitute. $(n-1)s^2 = 100$, so the interval is $\left(\dfrac{100}{21.920},\ \dfrac{100}{3.816}\right) = (4.5620,\ 26.2055)$. It is wide and strongly asymmetric — that is normal for a variance interval from only 12 observations.
  6. (c) Width is proportional to $1/\sqrt{n}$. To halve the width you need $\sqrt{n}$ to double, so $n$ must quadruple: $n \approx 4 \times 12 = 48$. (Slightly fewer in truth, because $t$ shrinks towards $z$ as $df$ grows, but 48 is the answer expected.)
a. (22.2966, 27.7034) · b. (4.5620, 26.2055) · c. n ≈ 48
Question 5 — Proportion test and Type II error 4+3 marks
p-value method, then the probability of missing a real shift

A manufacturer claims that at most 5% of the items produced by a machine are defective. In a random sample of 300 items, 22 were found defective.

a. Test the claim at 1% level of significance using the p-value method.

b. Using the same test, find the probability of a Type II error if the true defective rate is in fact 8%.

  1. (a) Hypotheses. "At most 5%" contains equality, so it is the null; the sample is above it, so the alternative points right: $$H_0: p \le 0.05 \qquad H_1: p > 0.05$$
  2. Sample proportion and standard error. $\hat{p} = \dfrac{22}{300} = 0.0733$, and under $H_0$ $$\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.05 \times 0.95}{300}} = \sqrt{0.00015833} = 0.0126$$
  3. Test statistic and p-value. $z = \dfrac{0.0733 - 0.05}{0.0126} = 1.85$, so the right-tail p-value is $1 - \Phi(1.85) = 1 - 0.9678 = 0.0322$.
  4. Decide. $0.0322 > 0.01$, so do not reject $H_0$ at the 1% level. There is not enough evidence at 1% to say the defective rate exceeds 5%. (Note it would have been rejected at 5% — the significance level genuinely decides this one.)
  5. (b) Find the decision boundary in $\hat{p}$ units. A Type II error means failing to reject when $H_1$ is true, so first fix the critical value. At $\alpha = 0.01$ right-tailed, $z_{0.01} = 2.33$: $$\hat{p}_{crit} = 0.05 + 2.33(0.012583) = 0.05 + 0.029319 = 0.0793$$ We reject only if $\hat{p} > 0.0793$.
  6. Re-centre on the true value $p = 0.08$. The standard error changes because $p$ changed: $$\sigma_{\hat{p}}' = \sqrt{\frac{0.08 \times 0.92}{300}} = \sqrt{0.00024533} = 0.0157$$
  7. β is the probability of landing in the acceptance region. $$\beta = P(\hat{p} < 0.0793 \mid p = 0.08) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{0.0793 - 0.08}{0.015663}\right) = \Phi(-0.04) = 0.4840$$
  8. Interpret. Even though the true rate is 8%, this test misses it about 48% of the time. The power is only $1 - \beta = 0.5160$. A 1% significance level with $n = 300$ is simply not sensitive enough to catch a shift this small.
a. z = 1.8543, p-value = 0.0322 > 0.01, do not reject H₀ · b. β = 0.4840 (power = 0.5160)
Question 6 — Variance test, one-tailed 4+2 marks
Chi-square in one tail, and squeezing a p-value out of a table

A bottling process is regarded as being in control if the variance of the fill weights does not exceed 1.5 g². A random sample of 16 bottles gives a sample variance of 2.8 g².

a. Test at 5% level whether the process variance exceeds 1.5 g².

b. Using the chi-square table only, place the p-value between two bounds.

  1. (a) Hypotheses. "Does not exceed" contains equality and is the in-control state: $$H_0: \sigma^2 \le 1.5 \qquad H_1: \sigma^2 > 1.5$$ This is one-tailed to the right, so the whole 5% sits in the upper tail.
  2. Test statistic. $$\chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} = \frac{15 \times 2.8}{1.5} = \frac{42}{1.5} = 28.0000 \qquad df = 15$$
  3. Critical value. $\chi^2_{0.05,\,15} = 24.996$.
  4. Decide. $28.0000 > 24.996$, so reject $H_0$ at the 5% level. There is sufficient evidence that the process variance exceeds 1.5 g² — the process is out of control on consistency.
  5. (b) Locate 28.0000 in the $df = 15$ row. Reading along that row: $\chi^2_{0.025,\,15} = 27.488$ and $\chi^2_{0.010,\,15} = 30.578$.
  6. Sandwich it. Since $27.488 < 28.0000 < 30.578$, the upper-tail area lies between 0.010 and 0.025: $$0.010 < p\text{-value} < 0.025$$ This is consistent with part (a): the whole interval sits below 0.05, so rejection at 5% was never in doubt. It would also be rejected at 2.5%, but not at 1%.
a. χ² = 28.0000 > 24.996, reject H₀ · b. 0.010 < p-value < 0.025
Question 7 — Spearman rank correlation with ties 7 marks
Repeated values, mid-ranks, and the correction factor

Find the rank correlation coefficient between the marks in Statistics and the marks in Economics from the data given below.

Statistics 68726875 80728568 7590
Economics 62706572 78688260 7488

Hint: look at the Statistics row before you start ranking.

  1. Spot the ties. In Statistics, 68 occurs three times, 72 occurs twice and 75 occurs twice. Economics has no repeats. Tied values must share the average of the ranks they would have occupied.
  2. Assign mid-ranks in Statistics. Sorted, the values are $68, 68, 68, 72, 72, 75, 75, 80, 85, 90$ occupying positions 1 to 10. The three 68s occupy positions 1, 2, 3, so each gets $\dfrac{1+2+3}{3} = 2$. The two 72s occupy 4 and 5, so each gets 4.5. The two 75s occupy 6 and 7, so each gets 6.5. Question 8 — Regression, correlation and prediction 2+2+2+2 marks
Identify the lines, recover σ, then predict with the right one

In a partially destroyed laboratory record of an analysis of bivariate data, the following results only are legible.

Legible $\operatorname{Var}(Y) = 4$, and the two regression lines are $$X + 4Y - 32 = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad X + Y - 11 = 0$$

a. The mean values of $X$ and $Y$.

b. Identify which line is which, and find the coefficient of correlation.

c. The standard deviation of $X$.

d. Estimate $Y$ when $X = 5$, stating clearly which line you must use and why.

  1. (a) Both lines pass through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. Solve $\bar{x} + 4\bar{y} = 32$ and $\bar{x} + \bar{y} = 11$. Subtracting the second from the first gives $3\bar{y} = 21$, so $\bar{y} = 7$, and then $\bar{x} = 11 - 7 = 4$.
  2. (b) Try the assignment and test $r^2 = b_{yx}b_{xy} \le 1$. Take $X + 4Y - 32 = 0$ as $Y$ on $X$: $4Y = 32 - X$ gives $b_{yx} = -\dfrac{1}{4} = -0.25$. Take $X + Y - 11 = 0$ as $X$ on $Y$: $X = 11 - Y$ gives $b_{xy} = -1$. Then $r^2 = (-0.25)(-1) = 0.25 \le 1$ — valid.
  3. Confirm the alternative is impossible. If $X + 4Y - 32 = 0$ were $X$ on $Y$ then $b_{xy} = -4$, and $X + Y - 11 = 0$ as $Y$ on $X$ gives $b_{yx} = -1$, so $r^2 = 4 > 1$. Rejected. The first assignment stands.
  4. Correlation. $r = \pm\sqrt{0.25} = \pm 0.5$, and both slopes are negative, so $r = -0.5000$.
  5. (c) Standard deviation of X. $\operatorname{Var}(Y) = 4$ gives $\sigma_y = 2$. From $b_{yx} = r\dfrac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$: $$-0.25 = -0.5 \times \frac{2}{\sigma_x} \;\Rightarrow\; -0.25 = \frac{-1}{\sigma_x} \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma_x = 4.0000$$
  6. (d) Choose the line by what is being predicted. We are given $X$ and want $Y$, so we must use the regression of $Y$ on $X$, which is $X + 4Y - 32 = 0$. The other line minimises horizontal deviations and would give a biased answer here.
  7. Substitute. $Y = \dfrac{32 - X}{4} = \dfrac{32 - 5}{4} = \dfrac{27}{4} = 6.7500$.
a. x̄ = 4, ȳ = 7 · b. X+4Y−32=0 is Y on X, r = −0.5000 · c. σ_x = 4.0000 · d. Ŷ = 6.7500
