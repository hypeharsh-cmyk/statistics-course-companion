Question 1 — Normal distribution 5 marks
Classification into divisions — the end-term's own Q1 pattern

In an examination it is laid down that a student passes if he secures 35 percent or more marks. He is placed in the first, second or third division according as he secures 60% or more marks, between 45% and 60% marks, and marks between 35% and 45% respectively. It is noticed from the result that 20% of the students failed in the examination, whereas 10% of them secured 70% or more marks.

Calculate the percentage of students placed in the first division and in the second division. (Assume the normal distribution of marks.)

Hint: the two facts you are given are the two percentages, not μ and σ. Recover μ and σ first, then every division is just a band.

  1. Translate the two facts into z-values. 20% failed means $P(X < 35) = 0.20$, so $z_1 = -0.84$. And 10% scored 70 or more means $P(X > 70) = 0.10$, i.e. $P(X < 70) = 0.90$, so $z_2 = 1.28$.
  2. Two standardising equations, two unknowns. $$\frac{35-\mu}{\sigma} = -0.84 \qquad \frac{70-\mu}{\sigma} = 1.28$$
  3. Subtract to eliminate μ. $\dfrac{70-35}{\sigma} = 1.28-(-0.84) = 2.12$, so $$\sigma = \frac{35}{2.12} = 16.5094$$
  4. Back-substitute for μ. $\mu = 35 + 0.84(16.5094) = 35 + 13.8679 = 48.8679$.
  5. First division is $X \ge 60$. $z = \dfrac{60-48.8679}{16.5094} = \dfrac{11.1321}{16.5094} = 0.67$, so $$P(X \ge 60) = 1 - \Phi(0.67) = 1 - 0.7486 = 0.2514$$
  6. Second division is $45 \le X < 60$. $z_{45} = \dfrac{45-48.8679}{16.5094} = -0.23$, and $\Phi(-0.23) = 0.4090$. So $$P(45 \le X < 60) = 0.7486 - 0.4090 = 0.3396$$
  7. Sense-check. Failed 20%, third division $= 0.4090-0.2000 = 20.90\%$, second 33.96%, first 25.14% — these sum to 100%, as they must.
μ = 48.8679, σ = 16.5094 · first division = 25.14% · second division = 33.96%
Question 2 — Uniform distribution and Chebyshev 5 marks
A bound that ignores the shape, against the truth

The waiting time $X$ (in minutes) for a shuttle bus is uniformly distributed over the interval $[0, 10]$.

Using Chebyshev's inequality, find an upper bound for $P\{|X-\mu| \ge 4\}$. Compute the exact probability from the uniform distribution and comment on the difference.

  1. Mean and variance of a uniform. For $X \sim U(a,b)$ with $a = 0$, $b = 10$: $$\mu = \frac{a+b}{2} = 5 \qquad \sigma^2 = \frac{(b-a)^2}{12} = \frac{100}{12} = 8.3333$$ so $\sigma = 2.8868$ minutes.
  2. Apply Chebyshev. With $c = 4$: $$P\{|X-5| \ge 4\} \le \frac{\sigma^2}{c^2} = \frac{8.3333}{16} = 0.5208$$ Equivalently $c = k\sigma$ gives $k = \dfrac{4}{2.8868} = 1.3856$ and $\dfrac{1}{k^2} = 0.5208$.
  3. Exact probability. $|X-5| \ge 4$ means $X \le 1$ or $X \ge 9$. For a uniform on $[0,10]$ the density is $\dfrac{1}{10}$, so $$P = \frac{1-0}{10} + \frac{10-9}{10} = 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2000$$
  4. Comment. The true probability 0.2000 is far below the Chebyshev ceiling 0.5208. The bound is valid but very loose here, because Chebyshev must cover every distribution with this mean and variance, including heavy-tailed ones. The uniform has no tails at all beyond its endpoints, so it sits comfortably inside the guarantee.
Chebyshev bound = 0.5208 · exact = 0.2000 · the bound holds and is loose
Question 3 — Sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$ 2+2+2 marks
Central Limit Theorem applied to a sample mean

According to a manufacturer, a certain brand of battery lasts on average 500 hours. Assume the population standard deviation is 64 hours. Suppose a random sample of 64 batteries is taken.

a. What is the probability that the sample mean is more than 512 hours?

b. What is the probability that the sample mean is less than 494 hours?

c. What is the probability that the sample mean is between 490 and 516 hours?

  1. Standard error first. $n = 64 \ge 30$, so by the Central Limit Theorem $\bar{X}$ is approximately normal with $$\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{64}{\sqrt{64}} = \frac{64}{8} = 8$$
  2. (a) $z = \dfrac{512-500}{8} = 1.50$. From the table $\Phi(1.50) = 0.9332$, so $P(\bar{X} > 512) = 1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668$.
  3. (b) $z = \dfrac{494-500}{8} = -0.75$, giving $P(\bar{X} < 494) = \Phi(-0.75) = 0.2266$.
  4. (c) $z_1 = \dfrac{490-500}{8} = -1.25$ and $z_2 = \dfrac{516-500}{8} = 2.00$. Then $P = \Phi(2.00) - \Phi(-1.25) = 0.9772 - 0.1056 = 0.8716$.
a. 0.0668 · b. 0.2266 · c. 0.8716
Question 4 — Confidence interval for μ 6 marks
σ unknown, small sample — the t-distribution

Let $\{2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12,\ 14\}$ be the set of collected random sample values from a $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ distribution, where both $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are unknown.

Find the 95% confidence interval for $\mu$.

  1. Sample mean. $\sum x = 56$, $n = 7$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{56}{7} = 8$.
  2. Sample variance. The deviations are $-6, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, 6$ and their squares are $36, 16, 4, 0, 4, 16, 36$, summing to $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 112$. Therefore $$s^2 = \frac{112}{7-1} = 18.6667 \qquad s = \sqrt{18.6667} = 4.3205$$
  3. Choose the tool. σ is unknown and $n = 7$ is small, so use $t$ with $n-1 = 6$ degrees of freedom.
  4. Critical value. For 95% two-sided, $t_{0.025,\,6} = 2.447$.
  5. Standard error and margin. $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \dfrac{4.3205}{\sqrt{7}} = 1.6330$, so the margin of error is $2.447 \times 1.6330 = 3.9959$.
  6. Assemble. $8 \pm 3.9959$.
95% CI for μ = (4.0041, 11.9959)
Question 5 — Test for a proportion 7 marks
p-value method, one-tailed

A political candidate claimed that more than 60% of the voters in a constituency support her. A random sample of 400 voters showed that 260 of them support her.

Test the claim of the candidate at 5% level of significance by using the p-value method.

  1. Hypotheses. The claim contains "more than", which has no equality in it, so the claim becomes the alternative: $$H_0: p \le 0.60 \qquad H_1: p > 0.60$$
  2. Sample proportion. $\hat{p} = \dfrac{260}{400} = 0.6500$.
  3. Standard error under $H_0$. Use the hypothesised $p_0 = 0.60$: $$\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.60 \times 0.40}{400}} = \sqrt{0.0006} = 0.0245$$
  4. Test statistic. $z = \dfrac{0.6500 - 0.60}{0.0245} = \dfrac{0.0500}{0.0245} = 2.04$.
  5. p-value. Right-tailed, so the p-value is the right tail: $P(Z > 2.04) = 1 - 0.9793 = 0.0207$.
  6. Decide. $0.0207 < 0.05$, so reject $H_0$ at the 5% level.
  7. Conclude in words. There is sufficient evidence at the 5% level that more than 60% of voters support the candidate. The claim is supported by the sample.
z = 2.0412 · p-value = 0.0207 < 0.05 · reject H₀, the claim is supported
Question 6 — Test for a population variance 6 marks
Chi-square test, two-tailed

The fill volumes (in millilitres, measured as deviations from the nominal value) of 8 randomly chosen bottles are given below.

25283027 32292631

Can we say that the population variance of the distribution of fill volumes, from which the above sample of 8 bottles was drawn, is equal to 2 square millilitres? Test an appropriate hypothesis at 5% level.

  1. Hypotheses. Two-tailed, because the question asks whether it equals 2: $$H_0: \sigma^2 = 2 \qquad H_1: \sigma^2 \ne 2$$
  2. Sample mean. $\sum x = 228$, so $\bar{x} = \dfrac{228}{8} = 28.5$.
  3. Sum of squared deviations. The deviations are $-3.5, -0.5, 1.5, -1.5, 3.5, 0.5, -2.5, 2.5$ and their squares are $12.25, 0.25, 2.25, 2.25, 12.25, 0.25, 6.25, 6.25$, so $\sum(x-\bar{x})^2 = 42$ and $s^2 = \dfrac{42}{7} = 6$.
  4. Test statistic. $$\chi^2 = \frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} = \frac{42}{2} = 21.0000 \qquad \text{with } df = 7$$
  5. Critical values. Two-tailed at 5%: $\chi^2_{0.975,\,7} = 1.690$ and $\chi^2_{0.025,\,7} = 16.013$.
  6. Decide. $21.0000 > 16.013$, so the statistic falls in the right rejection region. Reject $H_0$.
  7. Conclude. At the 5% level there is sufficient evidence that the population variance of fill volumes is not equal to 2 square millilitres; the sample suggests it is larger.
χ² = 21.0000, df = 7 · 21.0000 > 16.013 · reject H₀

Question 8 — Regression and correlation 2+2+2+2 marks
Recovering everything from two regression lines

In a partially destroyed laboratory record of an analysis of bivariate data, the following results only are legible.

Legible $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 16$, and the two regression lines are $$6X + 5Y - 240 = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 15X + 8Y - 465 = 0$$

a. The mean values of $X$ and $Y$.

b. Which line is which, with justification.

c. The coefficient of correlation between $X$ and $Y$.

d. The standard deviation of $Y$.

  1. (a) Both lines pass through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. Solve $6\bar{x} + 5\bar{y} = 240$ and $15\bar{x} + 8\bar{y} = 465$ simultaneously. Multiply the first by 15 and the second by 6: $$90\bar{x} + 75\bar{y} = 3600 \qquad 90\bar{x} + 48\bar{y} = 2790$$ Subtracting, $27\bar{y} = 810$, so $\bar{y} = 30$. Then $6\bar{x} = 240 - 5(30) = 90$, giving $\bar{x} = 15$.
  2. (b) Test the assignment with $r^2 = b_{yx}\,b_{xy} \le 1$. Take the first line as $Y$ on $X$: $5Y = 240 - 6X$ gives $b_{yx} = -\dfrac{6}{5} = -1.2$. Take the second as $X$ on $Y$: $15X = 465 - 8Y$ gives $b_{xy} = -\dfrac{8}{15} = -0.5333$. Then $r^2 = (-1.2)(-0.5333) = 0.64 \le 1$ — valid.
  3. Check the other way round fails. If the first were $X$ on $Y$ then $b_{xy} = -\dfrac{5}{6}$, and the second as $Y$ on $X$ gives $b_{yx} = -\dfrac{15}{8}$, so $r^2 = \dfrac{5}{6} \times \dfrac{15}{8} = 1.5625 > 1$, which is impossible.
  4. (c) Correlation. $r = \pm\sqrt{0.64} = \pm 0.8$. Both slopes are negative, so $r$ takes the negative sign: $r = -0.8000$.
  5. (d) Standard deviation of Y. $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 16$ gives $\sigma_x = 4$. From $b_{yx} = r\dfrac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$: $$-1.2 = -0.8 \times \frac{\sigma_y}{4} \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma_y = \frac{-1.2 \times 4}{-0.8} = 6.0000$$
a. x̄ = 15, ȳ = 30 · b. 6X+5Y−240=0 is Y on X · c. r = −0.8000 · d. σ_y = 6.0000
